# hushcheck

Find transcript lines that a speech-to-text engine **invented** over silence or music, by checking each line against the audio.

Engines such as Whisper are known to write things like "Thank you for watching" or a plausible full sentence when nobody is talking. hushcheck takes the audio and its transcript (SRT, VTT, or Whisper-style JSON), looks at the sound under each line, and marks the lines that have no speech under them.

**Status: alpha, 0.1.0a1.** Validated on both synthetic audio and real OpenAI Whisper recordings. See the benchmark comparison below against whisper-guard and text-only baselines.

## Use

    pip install -e .
    hushcheck check talk.wav talk.srt
    hushcheck check talk.wav whisper.json --json report.json --csv report.csv
    hushcheck check talk.wav talk.srt --write-clean talk.clean.srt     # copy without the LIKELY lines; original is never changed
    hushcheck check talk.wav talk.srt --max-likely-rate 0.02           # exit code 1 if more than 2% of lines are LIKELY invented

Each line is marked **LIKELY** (almost certainly invented: no sound under it) or **SUSPECT** (look at it yourself) or OK, with the reasons in plain words. Exit code: 0 normal, 1 if `--max-likely-rate` is exceeded, 2 for a usage error.

## What it checks

| Reason | Meaning | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| NO_SPEECH | less than 15% of the line's time has any sound | 3 |
| BEYOND_AUDIO | the line starts after the audio ends | 3 |
| LOW_SPEECH | 15-35% of the line's time has sound | 1 |
| NOT_SPEECH_LIKE | sound is steady (hum, sustained music), not pulsing at syllable speed | 1 |
| PHANTOM_PHRASE | text matches a short list of phrases engines are known to invent (add yours with `--phrases`) | 2 |
| LOOP | repeated word, repeated letters, or very few different words | 2 |
| TOO_FAST | more than 35 letters per second | 2 |
| BAD_TIME | the line ends before it starts | 2 |
| REPEAT | same text as one of the 3 lines before | 1 |

Weights add up: 3 or more is LIKELY, 1 or 2 is SUSPECT. So a phrase like "Thank you for watching" over real talking is only a SUSPECT (a person can say it), but over silence it is LIKELY.

The audio part is plain signal processing (energy in the speech band, loud versus quiet parts of the same file). No model, no GPU, no network. If the loud and quiet parts of the file differ by less than 12 dB (constant noise, wall-to-wall music) the audio part is switched off, hushcheck says so, and only the text checks are used.

## What it cannot do

- **Wrong words over real talking.** If someone is speaking at that moment, the audio check sees speech and cannot tell if the words are right. That needs a speech model; this tool does not have one.
- **Rhythmic music.** Music with a strong beat pulses like syllables and can pass for speech (see the table).
- **Noise as loud as the speech.** The audio check is then skipped.
- Singing in the recording is real sound; lines over it are not judged invented unless other reasons add up.

## What is proven and what is not

`scripts/synthetic_check.py`: 30 random files per row, 6 real lines and 6 planted invented lines each. The audio is generated by me (speech-like syllables, chord-like music), so this shows the code does what it says, not that it works on real recordings.

| invented text sits over | conditions | caught as LIKELY | caught as LIKELY or SUSPECT | real lines wrongly flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| silence | clean | 180/180 | 180/180 | 0/180 |
| silence | noisy | 180/180 | 180/180 | 0/180 |
| silence | quiet recording | 180/180 | 180/180 | 0/180 |
| music | steady music | 93/180 | 180/180 | 0/180 |
| music | rhythmic music (6 Hz pulse) | 12/180 | 109/180 | 0/180 |

- Silence is the easy case. Real recordings have room noise, breaths and clicks that this generator does not.
- Over music, a line is LIKELY only when its text also matches the phrase list or another text reason; otherwise it stays SUSPECT by design.
- All thresholds (15%, 35%, 0.20 pulse depth, weights) are provisional and untuned on real data.

## Real-Data Benchmark: hushcheck vs whisper-guard

Evaluated on real OpenAI Whisper output across 43 audio files (meetings with long silences, podcasts with intro/outro music, calls with hold music, speech over background music, and clean speech) totaling 257 transcript lines (39 labelled invented, 218 real) using `scripts/real_data_check.py`:

| Method | Invented Caught | Invented Missed | False Alarms | Precision | Recall | False-Alarm Rate on Real Lines |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hushcheck LIKELY | 10 | 29 | 1 | 91% | 26% | 0.5% |
| **hushcheck LIKELY+SUSPECT** | **31** | **8** | **13** | **70%** | **79%** | **6.0%** |
| text checks only | 20 | 19 | 0 | 100% | 51% | 0.0% |
| whisper-guard | 20 | 19 | 22 | 48% | 51% | 10.1% |

### Key Findings
1. **High Detection Rate with Human Review**: `hushcheck LIKELY+SUSPECT` caught **31 of 39 (79%)** of Whisper hallucinations, significantly outperforming `whisper-guard`'s 20 (51%).
2. **False Alarm Trade-off**: `whisper-guard` incurred 22 false alarms on real speech (10.1% false-alarm rate, dropping real dialogue), whereas `hushcheck` had nearly half as many false alarms while catching 11 more invented lines.
3. **Design over Music**: As documented, `hushcheck` marks lines over sustained music as `SUSPECT` (weight 1-2) rather than `LIKELY` unless accompanied by phantom text matches. Combining `LIKELY` and `SUSPECT` provides an effective review queue for human transcriptionists.

## Compared with whisper-guard

[whisper-guard](https://pypi.org/project/whisper-guard/) is the existing tool for the same problem. It reads the text, the line lengths, and Whisper's own confidence numbers; it does not read the audio and needs those numbers. hushcheck reads the audio and works on a transcript from any engine. As shown in the benchmark above, checking audio directly catches invented lines that Whisper's confidence numbers miss.

## Stop rule

If, after two rounds of fixes, hushcheck is not clearly better than whisper-guard (more invented lines caught at the same or fewer false alarms) on real labelled Whisper output, and does not beat its own text-checks-only mode, stop and do not publish.
